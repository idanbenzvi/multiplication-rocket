# The afterburner: a loop the loop, explained (the cockpit's easter egg)

The user, 2026-10-28: map one of the cockpit's unused buttons as an easter egg. Clicked, the
screen splits diagonally and the ship boosts itself round a 360° loop in space, while the newly
opened part of the screen shows a wireframe of the ship explaining every physical force on it as
it goes. Japanese anime style, with all the extreme speed effects.

## The button

The orange lever right of the joystick, two lights blinking by it (`boost` in `HOTSPOTS`,
screens.ts), named *Afterburner · do NOT pull!* (*מבער אחורי · לא למשוך!*), so a child will.
While the loop plays the cockpit can't be turned, nothing else in it can be clicked, the HUD on
the glass fades out and the TargetCursor is hidden (it forced a layout every frame).

## The story (loop/story.ts)

Told in beats, the way an anime tells a big move:

1. **charge** (480 ms): the engines charging, a red glow closing in at the screen's edges, focus
   lines (集中線) round the nose.
2. **slash** (140 ms): ズバッ, a glint along the cut.
3. **split** (520 ms): the blueprint slides in from beyond the far edge, overshooting a little
   (easeOutBack), its own edge the cut; the cockpit shifts aside to make room.
4. **ignite** (180 ms): ドォン!!, an impact frame (black, white lines, the blueprint inverted), the
   cockpit kicked and shuddering, the lens flashing.
5. Then the loop in **bursts**, each stopping dead in a **hold**: time frozen outside (the stars
   barely creeping, the colour drained), キラッ, the camera drifting round the ship, and a caption
   explaining one force. A tap (or Space, or Enter) goes on; the holds are long enough for a child
   to read (`holdFor`: 3.4–7.5 s by the words' length).
   - **thrust**, halfway along the run-up: the engine pushes the ship faster and faster.
   - **turn**, at 70°: to fly in a circle something has to push towards its middle; no air to
     lean on, so the side thrusters push.
   - **g**, at the top, upside down: space has no up or down; the seat pushes the pilot towards the
     middle, 3 G.
   - **inertia**, at 280°: if the thrusters stopped, the ship would fly straight on (the dashed
     line, a ghost ship on it).
   - **done**, out of the loop: ババーン! *Turning force = mass × speed² ÷ radius.*
6. **close** (560 ms): the blueprint slides away, the cockpit back as it was.

**Skip** (or Escape, or Back) jumps to the close. Each segment's entry (its sound, its effect) fires
only for the segment landed in, so skipping fires nothing it passes.

## The physics (honest numbers)

A real loop's: a 765 m circle at 150 m/s (540 km/h) needs 150² ÷ 765 ≈ 29.4 m/s², 3 g. The run-up
is 2 g of thrust from 40 m/s (v² growing with the distance, as a steady push does); out of the loop
the engine's lit again at 1.5 g. In deep space there's no air (no drag, no lift, no banking on it)
and next to no gravity: the engine is off round the loop and the side thrusters do all the turning
(the readouts say so: *No air out here: no drag, no lift · gravity ≈ 0*). An inside loop: the
canopy always faces the middle, so the pilot is pushed into the seat the whole way round.

The path is one number, s: −1 … 0 the run-up, 0 … 1 the loop (s · 360°), past 1 the way out;
`poseAt`, `speedAt`, `forcesAt` and `feltG` are pure functions of it.

## The split (loop/split.ts)

Wide: the cut runs from the top a little right of the middle (66%) to the bottom a little left of
it (38%), the blueprint on the right. Upright: across, from 52% down on the left to 36% on the
right, the blueprint below, the caption over the cockpit's top (under the notch) and the readouts
along the blueprint's foot.

## The blueprint (loop/wireframe.ts, loop/paint.ts)

On a 2D canvas over everything, projected by hand: a blueprint (deep blue, a grid, screentone)
with the loop's path (untravelled dotted, travelled bright, angle ticks, its centre), and the
pilot's own ship as a wireframe: the classic rocket (three fins, a porthole, a stripe), the
Explorer (an egg, a visor, four swept legs) or Venus (a round window, filigree, curved legs),
lathed into rings and ribs, all the same length. The pilot sits in its window, their avatar in a
glass bubble, squashed by the g. Arrows for the forces (thrust orange, turning force magenta,
velocity green, the seat gold, inertia's dashed path and ghost), sized to the camera's distance;
the flame and the side thrusters' puffs show which is pushing; the readouts (speed in km/h, g,
angle) top right. Speed lines and two afterimages in the bursts; in the holds the camera closes in,
orbits and rolls (a Dutch tilt), a burst of lines behind the ship.

## The cockpit side (loop/LoopShow.tsx, loop/live.ts)

The real cockpit keeps flying: the sky wheels round as the ship loops (`loopFx.pitch` turns the
nebula, stars, planets and comet in Space.tsx), the streaks rush with the bursts and nearly stop
in the holds (`loopFx.time`, `loopFx.warp`), the lens smears and splits (`fx.speed`, `fx.split`),
drains to grey when time stops (`fx.mono`), and darkens at the edges with the g (`fx.vignette`,
tunnel vision). The view shakes with the engine (`view.jolt`), the light glows from behind (the
engine) or below (the thrusters) (`glowLights.hot`), and the wind roars (`canyonWind`).

### The seat and the wheel (2026-10-29)

The user: make the cockpit move a little with the motion, reflecting the direction of
acceleration, and make the stars smear round the loop rather than in straight lines.

**The pilot in the seat.** The pilot's head is a mass on a spring (9 rad/s, damping 0.36), thrown
against the push: the engine's pushes it back (2 g on the run-up, 1.5 g out of the loop), the
turning force (towards the loop's middle, overhead) presses it down (3 g), 0.13 units a g, harder
in a burst (the push × 0.4–1 with the burst's speed). It overshoots and springs back as the push
changes (into the loop, out of it), and it steps with the flight's time, so it freezes mid-sway
when time stops. It moves the camera itself (`view.head`, Scene.tsx), so the cockpit (10 round)
shifts against the stars rather than everything moving together; the head tips with it a little
(pressed down, forwards; pushed back, back). The lens's glass mask follows the moved camera (the
ray to the cockpit's walls, Effects.tsx's `glassAt`). With reduced motion it stays still.

**The wheel.** Seen from the seat, a loop turns the sky round the ship's level axis, whose ends are
to the left and right, so the true smear is near-vertical. Three looks were drawn as mockups
(arcs round the turning axis, warp streaks bending as they burst from the nose, a spin round the
windscreen's middle) and the user chose the first: the anime's wide lens brings the axis's ends
(the hubs) in to just beyond the windscreen's sides (in the cockpit's half on a wide screen, at
the screen's edges upright), and everything sweeps down round them, straight under the nose and
curving more and more towards each side, a ) and a (. It's a Möbius turn with the hubs fixed
(`wheelAt`, live.ts, and the same in the shader): each point keeps to its circle round the hubs.
The lens smears the sky through the glass along those arcs (12 taps, `fx.wheel`), and each warp
streak's trail, now five pieces, goes back along its way and turns round the wheel the further
back it is (`loopFx.wheel`), so the streaks sweep the same way. How far round follows how fast the
sky's actually wheeling (smoothed, frozen when time stops), with a little kept round the loop so
the frozen moments still curve. Two simpler versions were tried first and dropped: a turn of the
sky round its true axis (straight vertical bars), and trails bent round the loop's middle in 3D
(the far streaks, turning as far as the near ones over far longer paths, fell as a vertical
curtain). With the CPU slowed 4× the loop runs as before (about 47 fps in, 53 to 55 round it).

The sound effects are written as manga writes them, in katakana (Dela Gothic One, only the
characters used, fetched as a subset), each with a small fansub's note in the pilot's language
(ズバッ *SLASH!*, ドォン!! *BOOM!!*, ギュイイン *VROOOM*, キラッ *TIME STOPS*, ババーン! *TA-DAA!*);
the captions are white manga boxes, skewed, in Bangers. New synth sounds in sfx.ts: `slash`,
`ignite`, `timeStop`, `loopDone`.

With reduced motion: no shakes, kicks, impact frame, lens smear, tunnel vision, speed lines,
afterimages or Dutch tilt; the panel slides without overshooting and the stars rush less.

## Words

English and Hebrew (`loop` in strings.ts); the canvas draws Hebrew right to left, its labels and
values swapped.

## Performance

A production build with the CPU slowed 4×: the cockpit alone 60 fps; the loop's intro (charge,
slash, split, ignite) about 47, the frozen holds about 50, the bursts and holds overall about 53.5.
Unslowed, 60 throughout. What it took: the TargetCursor hidden while looping, the path's dashes
drawn as dots, each wireframe part projected once a frame (`projectPart`), no `shadowBlur` (a
wider faint stroke under each arrow instead), the speed lines batched into four paths, two
afterimages rather than more, and the background (gradient, grid, screentone) cached.
