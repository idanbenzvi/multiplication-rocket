# The cockpit, all the way round

## Goal

⚙️ flies the pilot into their rocket's cockpit, and the cockpit is where everything that isn't
the flight lives: the game modes, the practice map, the badges, the pilot, the sound and every
setting. It's drawn from the user's own art, a NASA-punk 360° panorama (Gemini, `nasa_punk_360`),
with the game's screens on its screens, and it stays alive: the flight goes on outside the glass
(the stars streaking past, the pilot's numbers floating by, the destination ahead, as the old
settings cockpit had them), with a HUD on the windscreen counting down to the goal, now and then
a shudder, a comet or a surge of the drive. Looking round is a drag (or the mouse resting at an
edge); on a phone or a tablet, arrows step from one screen to the next. Touching a screen zooms
into it, the edges blurring radially with a vignette, and its page opens on it.

On a phone or a tablet the top bar keeps only the pilot and ⚙️; a computer keeps all its buttons.
The demo this follows is `~/sourcecode/equicode` (a sphere with the picture, `Html` panels on it).

## The picture (scripts/cockpit-360.py → public/cockpit/nasa-punk-360.webp)

- **The glass.** Six windows (the windscreen, two side windows, three canopy panes) are the black
  joined up with a point on each pane. The left window's black runs on round the bezels of the
  screens beside it, so thin strokes are opened away first; each window keeps its outermost 2 px
  (the ink line round its frame). Glass is alpha 0, with a pixel of soft edge. 2912 × 1440, 727 KB.
- **The screens.** Seven black screens, their corners measured as the extreme points along the
  diagonals (screens.ts). The bank of four on the left wall, the console screen, the tall right
  screen. The lit buttons were found as small bright saturated blobs, then picked by hand (the
  finder also found strap buckles).

## Geometry (geometry.ts, tested)

A point (u, v) of the panorama is a direction: yaw `(u - 0.5) · 2π` (right of ahead), pitch
`(0.5 - v) · π`; ahead is -z. A screen's four corners are four directions, and `screenQuad` puts
the parallelogram on them whose corners lie on those rays (with P0 + P2 = P1 + P3 and t0 = 1 it's
three linear equations). The art turns out consistent: every screen comes out within 7° of a
rectangle. `frame` gives the look and field of view that fill the view with a quad; `screenBox`
where it lands on screen.

## Drawing it

- **The shell** (Shell.tsx): a sphere round the camera whose shader looks the picture up by the
  direction of each pixel (no seam, nothing squashed at the poles; no mipmaps, as it's only ever
  magnified). It lights the walls: each screen's glow (brighter hovered or zoomed into), the HUD's,
  the hotspot under the hand, something passing outside, the lit buttons blinking (bright enough to
  bloom), and a shudder's flicker. Through the glass, a faint tint and a glint or two.
- **Space** (Space.tsx), drawn first, all of it beyond the cockpit's 10 units: a nebula, stars,
  streaks along the ship's nose (10° up, the windscreen's middle) at the flight's own speed
  (`flight.effective`, frozen as the game pauses) plus the drive's surges, the numbers as sprites
  (glyphs.ts, CockpitSpace's drawing moved here), the destination as a sprite drawn by drawPlanet,
  and a comet across the top of the windscreen.
- **The lens** (Effects.tsx): bloom over white, and one effect for the zoom's radial smear (from
  the field of view's rate of change), the turn's sideways smear, the edges blurred and darkened
  while zoomed in, a little colour split and film grain.
- **The screens' pages** (css3d.ts): three's CSS3DRenderer, a page per screen laid on its quad a
  thousand px away (CSS 3D likes distance), its size from the quad's shape, drawn each frame after
  the composer with the same camera, so they turn, zoom and shudder with the picture. The HUD on
  the windscreen is one, and the joystick, the overhead panel and the remote are invisible pads.
  What's behind the camera is hidden (CSS 3D draws it inside out). The faces (faces.tsx) show the
  gist live; the windscreen's HUD is WindscreenHud.tsx.
- **The view** (view.ts): yaw, pitch and field of view on critically damped springs, a drag
  holding the look and letting go with a fling, the edge-pan, the head following the mouse a
  little, and the shudders (every 6-14 s, sometimes twice) with the engine's faint hum.

## Getting round (useLookControls.ts, store.ts, Hud.tsx)

The stops, in the order a head turning right comes to them: ahead, the modes, the pilot, the
sound, the badges, the systems, the map. The arrows (and ← →, reversed in Hebrew) step through
them, each zooming into its screen; the heading tape along the top turns to the three walls; with a
mouse there's a dock down the left edge, and React Bits' TargetCursor locks onto whatever's under
it. A drag that starts on a screen doesn't click it.

## A screen's page (Panel.tsx, pages/)

It opens once the zoom has (nearly) arrived, right over the screen's glass where the zoomed view
has it, powering on like a tube (a dot, a line, the whole screen), its title decrypting. Where
the glass is too small for it (a small screen on a phone), it grows out of it to fill the view,
the arrows under it, or beside it on a phone on its side. The pages reuse the game's own:
`useGameModes` (out of GameModes.tsx), `MasteryMap` (out of MasteryHeatmap.tsx), `BadgesContent`
(out of BadgesPage.tsx). The pilots and About are opened by their own top-bar buttons on request
(pages.ts, from the phone-menu branch), which stay mounted, out of sight, on a phone.

## Layering

The cockpit is at z 25: over the game and its top bar, under the launch bay (26, for the pilots
opened from it), the parents' dialogs (30, 35), the pause screen (50) and About (60). While any of
those is over it, it stops drawing (`usePause().why`). Starting a game mode dismisses it (the music
back, the game running) before the mode's own start, so the rocket blasts off into the warp.

## Gone

The settings cockpit's toy dashboard (Cockpit.tsx and its controls, droids, plates, textures) and
the sideways phone's menu drawer (a top bar of two buttons doesn't need one).
